05 / The turn
A better question

STOP ASKING
“WHY IS IT LATE?”
ASK “WHERE DID THE
WAIT HAPPEN?”

The data gap is the insight. Public city datasets describe delivery activity and street context—but do not publicly split an individual order into kitchen, courier pickup, and travel stages.

What needs to change

Measure order events consistently. Show stage-based status updates to customers. Give restaurants and platform operations a shared, aggregated view of pickup handoffs.

What must not happen

Do not claim that a specific stage causes most NYC delays without order-level timestamps. Do not expose private courier information or use the pilot to push riders to travel faster.

06 / The ask — Pilot proposal

SHOW WHERE
THE WAIT HAPPENS.

A four-week NYC pilot can test whether clearer order stages reduce unexplained waits for customers and improve coordination without increasing pressure on couriers.

01

Timestamp the handoffs

Record accepted, food-ready, courier-arrival, pickup and drop-off events when available.

02

Explain the stage

Show honest customer-facing status labels instead of only a changing ETA.

03

Evaluate responsibly

Compare clarity, data completeness and aggregate pickup waits, with worker privacy safeguards.
